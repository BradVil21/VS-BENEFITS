The Florida small business tax credit — do you qualify?

Many Florida owners overpay because they don't know about the federal Small Business Health Care Tax Credit. It's worth up to 50% of what you contribute toward employee premiums. You may qualify if you:

  • Have fewer than 25 full-time-equivalent employees
  • Pay average annual wages under about $62,000 (the cap is adjusted for inflation each year)
  • Contribute at least 50% of each employee's premium
  • Buy coverage through the SHOP Marketplace — which we set up for you

The credit can be claimed for two consecutive years, and your premium contributions are generally tax-deductible on top of it. We'll flag whether you're eligible when we quote you. For the full breakdown, read the Florida small business tax credit guide. (We're insurance advisors, not tax advisors — confirm specifics with your CPA.)

Florida small business health insurance FAQ

How much does small business health insurance cost in Florida in 2026?

For 2026, employee-only group premiums in Florida typically run about $510–$720/month for a Silver HMO and $590–$850/month for a Silver PPO, before any employer contribution. Your actual rate depends on employee ages, ZIP code, the plan chosen, and your contribution level. We shop every major Florida carrier to find your lowest quote at no cost — get your real numbers here.

How many employees do I need to qualify?

In Florida a small group is generally 1–50 employees, and most carriers require at least 2 enrolled employees, usually including one non-owner W-2 employee. We place groups from 2 to 50 employees.

Can my Florida business get a tax credit for offering coverage?

Possibly. The federal Small Business Health Care Tax Credit is worth up to 50% of your premium contributions if you have fewer than 25 full-time-equivalent employees, pay average annual wages under about $62,000 (indexed each year), contribute at least 50% of employee-only premiums, and enroll through the SHOP Marketplace. It's available for two consecutive years. Confirm specifics with your tax advisor.

When can I start a plan?

Any month. Group health insurance has no open enrollment window, so you don't have to wait — you can set up coverage for your Florida team year-round.

Do I have to pay the whole premium for my employees?

No. Most Florida carriers require the employer to cover at least 50% of the employee-only premium. Dependent coverage is usually optional for the employer to fund. We'll show you the minimum and help you build a package that fits your budget.

Which carrier is best in my area?

It depends on where your employees live — networks vary by county. Florida Blue reaches all 67 counties, while Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, Aetna and AvMed are strong in specific metros. We match you to the best network for your team.
