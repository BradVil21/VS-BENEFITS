Why self-employed people and high earners look at private plans

If you are paying the full Marketplace price anyway, the subsidy is no longer a reason to stay. Here is what the private side can offer instead.

Nationwide PPO networks

Major carriers' PPO networks reach across the country instead of stopping at a county or state line. In many states the exchange offers mostly HMO and EPO plans.

Coverage that travels with you

If you drive across states, split time between homes, or have a kid in college out of state, a national network means routine care, not just emergencies, away from home.

Pick your own doctors

PPOs let you see in-network specialists without a referral, and many pay part of out-of-network care too. We check your doctors against the network before you apply.

Priced on health and age, not income

Your premium does not move with your income, there is no subsidy cliff to fall off, and nothing to pay back at tax time. The trade-off: the carrier asks health questions.

Making too much for a subsidy? You are who this page is for

With the enhanced premium tax credits expired, the 400% of poverty level cutoff is back for 2027. Above it there is no credit at all: roughly $63,000 for a single adult and about $130,000 for a family of four. Cross that line by a dollar and you pay the full Marketplace price.

That is the range where a healthy applicant often finds a privately underwritten plan for less, with a better network. It is also why we run your subsidy number first: if you qualify for a meaningful credit, the Marketplace usually wins and we will say so.

Worth comparing if you are

  • Self-employed, a 1099 contractor or an owner-operator in reasonably good health
  • A family earning above the subsidy cutoff and paying full price
  • On the road, in two states, or covering a student away at school
  • Outside open enrollment with no qualifying life event
  • Retired early and bridging to Medicare

Probably better on the Marketplace if you

  • Qualify for a premium tax credit of any real size
  • Have an ongoing condition or take a maintenance medication
  • Are pregnant or planning a pregnancy this year
  • Want guaranteed acceptance with no health questions

The honest part: health questions

Private plans are cheaper for some people because the carrier gets to decide whom it covers. When you apply, it reviews your answers (and often your prescription history) and can respond in one of four ways:

  • Accept you at the quoted rate. The common outcome for applicants in good health.
  • Accept you at a higher rate. The quote you first see is for a standard risk.
  • Accept you with an exclusion. The policy is issued but a named condition is carved out.
  • Decline you. No policy is issued, and you can still enroll in a Marketplace plan during an enrollment window.

We go through the questions with you before anything is submitted, so the decision is not a surprise. And never cancel the coverage you have until a new policy is approved and issued in writing.
