I work with small employers around South Florida — trades, restaurants, salons, dental offices, trucking, retail — setting up health benefits for teams that never had them.

Most owners I talk to assume a group plan is either legally required or financially impossible. Usually neither is true:

•  Under 50 full-time-equivalent employees, you are not required to offer coverage.

•  A group plan can start with as few as 2 enrolled employees.

•  Carriers typically expect the employer to cover about half of the employee-only premium — not the family cost, not the whole thing.

•  Every dollar you contribute is a deductible business expense, and businesses under 25 employees with modest average wages may qualify for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit on top.

•  Coverage is guaranteed-issue — nobody on your team gets turned down for their health history.

The reason most small businesses skip it is not the money. It is that nobody wants to spend three weeks figuring out carriers, participation rules and paperwork while running a business.
