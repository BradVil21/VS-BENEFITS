How the Contribution Split Works

You do not pay the whole premium. Most small employers cover 50 to 80 percent of the employee-only rate (many carriers require at least half), and employees pick up the rest plus any family coverage they add. The split is your main budget dial: a generous split wins hiring battles, a leaner one still beats offering nothing.

The Tax Math That Shrinks the Real Cost

  • Premiums are deductible. Every dollar you contribute is a business expense, so the after-tax cost is meaningfully lower than the sticker.
  • The Small Business Health Care Tax Credit. Businesses under 25 full time equivalent employees with modest average wages that contribute at least half of premiums may get a credit covering a significant share of their cost.
  • Payroll advantages. Premiums run through the business avoid the payroll taxes that an equivalent raise would trigger, which is why a benefit dollar goes further than a wage dollar.

Five Ways to Lower the Cost

  1. Never renew blind. Carriers count on it. An annual re-shop routinely finds savings for the same coverage.
  2. Level funded plans. Healthy team? These can beat traditional group rates and refund unused claim dollars at year end.
  3. ICHRA or QSEHRA. Set a fixed monthly allowance per employee and cap your budget permanently. Details in our guide to offering coverage to employees.
  4. Tier strategy. A silver base plan with an optional buy-up often satisfies everyone for less than a rich plan nobody asked for.
  5. Right-size the split. Model 50, 60, and 75 percent contributions before deciding. We run those scenarios in every quote.

What You Get for the Money

Turnover is the hidden line item this benefit attacks. Replacing a trained employee costs months of productivity, recruiting spend, and training time, and benefits are consistently the deciding factor for candidates comparing offers. For most small businesses the honest comparison is not coverage versus nothing, it is coverage versus the cost of churn. Group Insurance Cost FAQ

How much does small business health insurance cost per employee?

Commonly $300 to $600 per employee per month at full premium. After a typical 50 percent employer split, the employer share often lands around $150 to $300 per employee per month, tax deductible.

What percentage do employers usually pay?

Most contribute 50 to 80 percent of the employee-only premium, with many carriers requiring at least half.

Are premiums tax deductible?

Yes, generally as a business expense, and eligible sub-25-employee businesses may also qualify for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit.

How can I lower the cost?

Re-shop annually, consider level funded plans, look at ICHRA and QSEHRA, adjust tiers, and model different contribution splits.

Is there an enrollment deadline for group plans?

No. Small group coverage can start any month of the year.
