# Contractor cold campaign — HVAC / plumbing / electrical / roofing

Built 17 Sep 2026. Audience: Florida home-improvement contractors, 10–60 W-2 employees.
Pitch: ICHRA for recruiting and retention. Same structure as the restaurant sequence,
different pain, different send times, new sending domain.

## The one-paragraph answer on timing

**Your window is October 6 through November 20. It is not December.**

Everything works backward from **December 15, 2026** — the last day someone can enroll in
individual coverage that starts **January 1, 2027**. Open enrollment runs Nov 1 2026 –
Jan 15 2027, but Jan 15 only buys a **February 1** start. (The shortened Dec-15-end
enrollment window HHS finalized in 2025 was vacated by a federal judge in June 2026, so
2027 is back on the normal calendar.)

Back it out:

| Date | What has to be true |
|---|---|
| **Dec 15, 2026** | Every employee has picked and enrolled in a plan |
| **Nov 24 – Dec 5** | Employees shop and enroll. They need ~2 weeks, not 2 days. |
| **Nov 10 – Nov 21** | Owner signs, classes are set, funding amount decided |
| **Oct 20 – Nov 14** | **Your calls happen here.** Discovery, quote, decision. |
| **Oct 6 – Nov 12** | **Your emails go out here.** Every email should land inside this. |
| **Now – Oct 3** | Domain warm-up and list build. No sending. |

A contractor who first hears from you December 1 will not have a plan on January 1.
Anything you send after about November 20 is really a February 1 conversation, and you
should sell it that way instead of pretending.

**The good news nobody tells you:** an ICHRA offer is itself a qualifying event. When an
employer newly offers one, employees get a 60-day special enrollment period to buy
individual coverage — so this sells year-round, not just in Q4. Q4 is just when the phone
gets answered, because everyone's already thinking about it.

### The 90-day notice thing — the honest version

ICHRA rules say employees get written notice **90 days before the plan year starts**.
For a Jan 1 2027 start that is **October 3, 2026**, which is two weeks out.

Do not use that as a hard deadline in your emails, because it isn't one: a **newly
established** ICHRA can deliver the notice as late as the first day of coverage. If you
tell an owner "you've missed it" and his CPA tells him otherwise, you've lost him.

What it *is*: your best reason to call in early October and sound like you know the
calendar. "The notice rule wants 90 days. First-year plans get an exception, but your
guys still need three weeks to actually pick something, so we should talk this month."

### When contractors actually read email

This is the biggest change from the restaurant sequence. Restaurant owners are at a desk
mid-morning. Contractors are not.

- **Send 6:15–7:15 AM, Tue / Wed / Thu.** That's the truck-loading, coffee, before-the-
  morning-huddle window. It is when an HVAC or roofing owner clears his inbox on his phone.
- Second-best: **4:30–6:00 PM**, after the last call clears.
- **Never** 9:00 AM – 3:00 PM. He's on a roof or in an attic and your email gets buried.
- **Never Monday** (dispatch chaos) **or Friday** (job closeout and payroll).

**Phone calls: 7:00–8:00 AM, or 4:30–6:00 PM.** Same logic. Early morning is better —
he's in the truck, he's not yet behind, and he'll talk.

### Seasonality, Florida specifically

- **HVAC** — May through September is the crush. October is when the owner comes up for
  air and has a minute. October is your month for HVAC.
- **Roofing** — hurricane season runs to Nov 30. If a storm hits, roofers vanish into
  reconstruction work and will not talk benefits until spring. If the season stays quiet,
  October and November are wide open and roofing is your best segment.
- **Plumbing / electrical** — flatter year. Less seasonal excuse either way.

## Plain text first — read this before you paste anything

Your restaurant templates are good HTML: no images, one link, inline styles. They were
also going out from a domain with history behind it.

The new cold domain has **zero reputation**. On a brand-new domain, a 600px HTML table
with a styled button is the single most campaign-looking thing you can send. For the
first ~60 days, send `EMAILS-plaintext.md` — typed-looking, no markup, link as bare text.
Plain text from a new domain outperforms HTML from a new domain by a wide margin, and it
matches the premise of the whole sequence, which is that a person typed it.

Switch to the HTML versions once the domain has 60 days of clean sending behind it, or
use them now from your existing warm setup.
