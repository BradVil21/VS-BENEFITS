Common Questions

PPO Health Insurance FAQ

What is a PPO health insurance plan?

A PPO, or Preferred Provider Organization, is a health insurance plan that lets you see any doctor or specialist without needing a referral. You pay less when you use in-network providers, but you still have coverage if you go out-of-network.

What is the difference between a PPO and an HMO?

A PPO gives you the freedom to see any provider without a referral, including out-of-network doctors. An HMO typically requires you to choose a primary care doctor and get referrals before seeing specialists. HMOs tend to cost less but offer significantly less flexibility.

Is a PPO plan worth the extra cost?

For people who travel, have specific specialists they want to keep, or value flexibility in their care, a PPO is often worth the higher premium. We compare the real cost difference and help you decide based on your actual situation, not a general rule.

Can self-employed workers and 1099 contractors get a PPO?

Yes. Self-employed individuals, 1099 workers, and small business owners can all access PPO plans through the ACA marketplace or the private market. VS Benefits compares all available options at no cost to you.

What is a private health insurance plan?

Private health insurance refers to coverage purchased outside of government programs like Medicaid or Medicare. This includes ACA marketplace plans, employer group plans, and off-marketplace individual plans. VS Benefits helps you navigate all of these options.
