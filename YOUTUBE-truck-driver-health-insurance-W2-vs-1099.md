## Script

**[0:00] Hook**
"If you drive a truck, the first question about health insurance isn't which company. It's this: are you W-2 or 1099?
Your answer changes where you buy, what you pay and how much help you get. I'm Bradley Vilsaint, a licensed health
insurance agent, and I help drivers in 40-plus states. Let's break it down in under five minutes."

**[0:35] W-2 company drivers**
"If you're a company driver, your carrier usually offers a group plan. Most bigger fleets offer medical, dental and
vision, and the company pays part of the premium. Three things to check before you enroll:
One, the network. If you run regional or OTR, a local HMO won't help you three states from home. Ask if it's a national PPO.
Two, the deductible and out-of-pocket max, not just the paycheck deduction.
Three, the cost to add your spouse and kids. That's where company plans get expensive."

**[1:30] The waiting-period gap**
"New carrier? Most plans start after a waiting period, often 60 to 90 days. Don't drive uninsured in that gap.
If you're coming off your old company's plan, losing it opens a 60-day window to buy a Marketplace plan, and right
now open enrollment is open to everyone. Use it as a bridge and cancel it when the new company plan starts.
COBRA from your old job is the other option, but it's usually much more expensive."

**[2:05] 1099 owner-operators**
"Owner-operators and lease operators are self-employed, so you buy your own plan. Here's what most drivers miss:
Marketplace tax credits are based on your NET income, after truck payments, fuel, insurance and maintenance.
A lot of owner-operators gross six figures but net much less, and that can mean a much lower premium than they expect.
Watch the 400 percent line: for 2027 coverage that's about $63,840 for a single person. Go a dollar over and the credit
is gone. Retirement or HSA contributions can bring you back under. And as self-employed, your premiums may be deductible.
Ask your tax pro."

**[3:05] The network problem**
"On the road, the network matters more than the price. The ER is covered anywhere in the country, but urgent care and
regular doctor visits out of state often aren't. Marketplace PPOs are scarce in a lot of states, so check the network
before you pick the cheapest plan. You buy in your home state, the one on your license and tax return."

**[3:45] 2027 deadlines**
"Open enrollment is open right now. In most states you have until January 15 to enroll. Pick a plan by December 15 if you
want coverage on January 1. And premiums went up again for 2027, so don't let your plan auto-renew without comparing."

1099, and I'll compare every plan for you, free. You can even do it by text from the cab. Drive safe."
