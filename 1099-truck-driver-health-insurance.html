1099 and Independent

1099 Truck Driver Health Insurance: Plans, Subsidies & Cost

A 1099 means no employer plan, but it also means you qualify for subsidies that company drivers cannot get, and you can deduct every dollar of your premium. Here is how to use both.

If your carrier hands you a 1099 instead of a W-2, you are an independent contractor. Legally, you are running your own business, and that has one immediate consequence: no employer is providing your health insurance, and nobody is going to.

You buy your own. That is more straightforward than it sounds, and it often works out better than the plan a company driver gets. But it puts the burden of choosing on you, and most drivers make the decision in a hurry with bad information.

What Being 1099 Actually Changes

W-2 Company Driver1099 Independent Driver
Who provides coverageThe carrier, usually with a payroll deductionYou do, on the individual market
Plan choiceWhatever the carrier pickedAny plan available in your state
Premium helpEmployer pays part of itACA premium tax credit, based on your income
Tax treatmentPre-tax payroll deductionSelf-employed health insurance deduction
What happens if you switch carriersYou lose the planNothing. The plan is yours and it moves with you.

That last row is worth sitting with. A 1099 driver's health plan is not tied to who they are hauling for this month. If you leave a carrier, change lanes, or go get your own authority, your coverage does not lapse. Company drivers lose their plan the day they leave. You do not.

Your Health Insurance Options as a 1099 Truck Driver

ACA marketplace plans

The main route, and usually the best one. You cannot be turned down or charged more for a pre-existing condition. Premium tax credits are calculated on your net self-employment income, which for a driver with real equipment expenses is often much lower than gross revenue.

Critically: as a 1099 driver you are almost always eligible for a subsidy, because you have no offer of employer coverage. Employees with an affordable employer plan available to them are locked out of subsidies. You are not. That is a genuine advantage of your status.

Private PPO plans

Sold off-marketplace, no subsidy, sometimes broader networks. Worth looking at if your income is high enough that you would not receive a meaningful tax credit anyway.

Short term medical

Useful for one thing only: bridging a genuine gap, such as the weeks between leaving a company driving job and getting your own coverage active. Understand what you are buying. Short term plans commonly exclude pre-existing conditions, may not cover prescriptions or maternity, and can decline to renew you. It is a bridge, not a destination.

A spouse's employer plan

Always check this before anything else. It takes one phone call and it sometimes beats everything else on the table.

If a company classifies you as 1099 but controls your schedule, your routes, and your equipment, you may actually be a misclassified employee under federal rules. That affects taxes and benefits. It is worth raising with an employment attorney if it sounds like your situation, because it can change what you are owed.

The 1099 Tax Advantage Nobody Explains

You get to deduct your health insurance premiums. The self-employed health insurance deduction lets you take 100 percent of what you pay for yourself, your spouse, and your dependents straight off your adjusted gross income (one reason your real cost runs well below the sticker price — see our truck driver health insurance cost breakdown), and you do not have to itemize to claim it.

There is a second effect worth understanding. Lowering your AGI can, in some situations, also affect the size of the premium tax credit you qualify for. The two interact. This is genuinely worth having a tax preparer look at rather than guessing, because the interaction can move real money.

One restriction: the deduction is not available for any month you were eligible for a subsidized health plan through an employer, including your spouse's employer. If your spouse has an affordable family plan available, that closes the door on this deduction even if you decline the plan.

When You Can Enroll

Open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs from November 1 to December 15 in most states. Outside that window you need a qualifying life event. For a 1099 driver, the ones that come up most often are:

  • Losing other coverage. Leaving a W-2 driving job and losing that plan is a qualifying event. So is aging off a parent's plan at 26.
  • Moving. A permanent move to a new county or state, including changing your domicile, can open a special enrollment period.
  • Marriage, divorce, or a new child. Birth and adoption both count.
  • An income change that alters your subsidy eligibility, in some circumstances.

You generally have 60 days from the qualifying event to enroll. Miss that window and you are typically waiting until the next open enrollment, which for a driver with no coverage is a genuinely bad place to be. If you have just had a qualifying event, do not sit on it.

What to Do Right Now

  • Find your net income, not your gross. Take last year's Schedule C, or estimate revenue minus fuel, maintenance, truck payment, insurance, tolls, and depreciation. That is the number that determines your subsidy.
  • Decide whether you need national network coverage. If you run OTR, the answer is yes, and that points you toward a PPO.
  • List your prescriptions. Check them against the plan formulary before you enroll, not after.
  • Compare total exposure, not premium. Premium times twelve plus the out-of-pocket maximum.
  • Do not leave a gap. If you are between carriers, get something in place, even if it is temporary.
Questions

1099 Truck Driver Insurance FAQ

Can a 1099 truck driver get health insurance?

Yes. As an independent contractor you buy individual coverage rather than employer coverage. ACA marketplace plans cannot deny you for a pre-existing condition, and because you have no offer of employer coverage you are almost always eligible for premium tax credits based on your income.

Do 1099 drivers qualify for ACA subsidies?

Usually yes, and often for more than they expect. Subsidies are calculated on net self-employment income after business deductions such as fuel, maintenance, truck payments, and depreciation. Drivers with high gross revenue frequently still qualify once real operating costs are subtracted.

Can 1099 truck drivers deduct health insurance premiums?

Generally yes. The self-employed health insurance deduction allows you to deduct 100 percent of premiums for yourself, your spouse, and dependents from adjusted gross income without itemizing. It is not available for months when you were eligible for a subsidized employer plan, including through a spouse. Confirm with your tax advisor.

What is the difference between 1099 and W-2 health insurance for drivers?

A W-2 company driver gets whatever plan the carrier chose and loses it when they leave. A 1099 driver chooses any plan available in their state, keeps it when they switch carriers, can receive ACA premium tax credits, and can deduct the premium. The 1099 driver has more control and the plan is portable.

What if I am between carriers and have no coverage?

Losing coverage is a qualifying life event, which opens a 60 day special enrollment period for a full ACA plan. If you need something immediately while that is processing, short term medical can bridge the gap, but be aware it commonly excludes pre-existing conditions and is not a long term substitute.

Do I need a plan that works in other states?

If you run OTR or regional, yes. Look for a PPO with a national provider network. HMO plans typically cover only emergencies outside their network area, which is a serious problem for a driver who is rarely in their home county.

When can a 1099 driver enroll?

During open enrollment, which runs November 1 to December 15 in most states for 2027 coverage. Outside that window you need a qualifying life event such as losing coverage, moving, marriage, or the birth of a child, and you generally have 60 days to act.

What health insurance do 1099 truck drivers need?

A 1099 truck driver needs an individual major-medical plan, ideally a PPO with a national network so you are covered across state lines. The ACA marketplace is the usual starting point because it offers income-based subsidies and cannot deny you for pre-existing conditions. Short-term and health-sharing plans exist but are not full coverage, so confirm exactly what you are buying before enrolling.

Is 1099 truck driver insurance more expensive than a company plan?

The sticker premium can look higher because you pay the full amount yourself instead of splitting it with an employer. But 1099 drivers get two offsets a company driver does not: ACA subsidies calculated on net self-employment income, and a full above-the-line deduction for premiums. After both, many 1099 truck drivers pay less than a comparable company plan.
