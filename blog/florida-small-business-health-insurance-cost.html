How Much Does Small Business Health Insurance Cost in Florida? (2026)

See 2026 Florida small business health insurance costs: real per-employee premium ranges for HMO and PPO group plans, what drives your rate, employer contribution rules, and how to save. Free quote.

Florida small business health insurance costs in 2026

For 2026, group health premiums in Florida rose about 12–18% — meaningfully less than the roughly 31% jump on the individual ACA marketplace. That gap is a big reason a group plan is often the smarter buy for a Florida team. Here's a realistic benchmark for what employee-only coverage costs per month, before your employer contribution:

2026 Florida monthly premium ranges (employee-only)

  • Silver HMO: about $510–$720 per employee
  • Silver PPO: about $590–$850 per employee
  • Employee + spouse (Silver PPO): about $1,100–$1,650
  • Family (Silver PPO): about $1,600–$2,400
  • Level-funded plans: often 10–25% below traditional for healthier groups

These are market ranges for illustration — your actual number depends on the specifics below. Want your real figure? Try the cost calculator or get a live quote.

What actually drives your cost

Two Florida businesses with the same headcount can get very different quotes. The variables carriers weigh most:

  • Employee ages and ZIP codes: Older teams and higher-cost counties (Miami-Dade, Monroe) price higher.
  • Plan tier and network: HMO vs PPO, and Bronze/Silver/Gold, move the premium substantially.
  • Your contribution level: How much of the premium you cover shapes both cost and participation.
  • Group claims history: For level-funded plans, a healthy group can unlock double-digit savings.
  • Participation rate: Most carriers want 70–75% of eligible employees enrolled.

How much do employers have to pay?

Florida has no state contribution mandate, but most carriers require the employer to fund at least 50% of the employee-only premium. In practice, competitive Florida employers cover 50–75% of employee-only cost and 25–50% of dependent coverage. Paying more helps recruiting and lifts participation — which can actually lower your per-person rate.

4 ways Florida employers lower the cost

  1. Claim the tax credit. If you have under 25 full-time-equivalent employees and modest average wages, the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit can offset up to 50% of your contributions.
  2. Consider level-funded. Healthy groups often save 10–25% and can get money back in low-claims years.
  3. Shop every carrier at renewal. Auto-renewing is the most common way Florida owners overpay. An independent advisor benchmarks 8–10 carriers.
  4. Use pre-tax structures. A Section 125 plan lets employees pay their share pre-tax and cuts your payroll taxes.

A quick Florida example

A 12-person Tampa contractor covering 60% of a Silver HMO (~$620/employee) budgets roughly $4,464/month in employer cost. Switching to a level-funded plan and claiming the tax credit trimmed their net cost by nearly 20% in year one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average cost of small business health insurance in Florida?

In 2026, employee-only group coverage in Florida typically runs about $510–$720/month for a Silver HMO and $590–$850 for a Silver PPO before the employer contribution. Your rate depends on ages, ZIP codes, plan, and contribution.

Is group insurance cheaper than individual coverage in Florida?

Often, yes — especially in 2026, when group rates rose far less than individual marketplace rates, and when you factor in the tax advantages available to employers.

How much does the employer have to contribute?

Most Florida carriers require at least 50% of the employee-only premium; many employers pay 50–75% to stay competitive and meet participation rules.
