Do Truck Drivers Get Health Insurance? A 2026 Guide for Company Drivers and Owner-Operators

Short answer: yes, truck drivers can absolutely get health insurance, and many pay far less than they expect. How you get it depends on whether you drive for a company or run your own authority as an owner-operator. This guide breaks down your options, what coverage really costs in 2026, and how to get a plan that works on the road.

Quick Answer

  • Company drivers may get coverage through their carrier, but many plans are limited or start after a waiting period.
  • Owner-operators and 1099 drivers buy their own plan through the ACA marketplace or a private PPO, and often qualify for subsidies.
  • Cost: Many owner-operators pay $0 to $80 per month after tax credits.
  • Best fit for the road: a PPO plan with a nationwide network, so you are covered in every state you run.

Company Driver or Owner-Operator? It Changes Everything

The single biggest factor in how you get health insurance as a trucker is your employment status.

Company drivers (W-2) may be offered a group health plan through their carrier. That is a real benefit, but read the fine print. Many trucking company plans have long waiting periods, narrow networks that do not travel well, or high costs for family coverage. If your company plan is weak or you are still in the waiting period, you may do better on your own plan.

Owner-operators and 1099 drivers are self-employed, so there is no employer plan. You buy your own coverage. The good news is that self-employed drivers often qualify for ACA premium tax credits that cut the cost dramatically, and your premium may be tax deductible as a business expense. Health Insurance Options for Truck Drivers

1. PPO plans (best for the road)

A PPO lets you see any doctor in any state without a referral. For long-haul and OTR drivers who are away from home for weeks, this is usually the best fit. Learn more on our OTR driver coverage page.

2. ACA marketplace plans

If your income qualifies, federal premium tax credits can bring your monthly cost way down. Many owner-operators and 1099 drivers pay between $0 and $80 per month after credits. We check every subsidy you are eligible for.

3. Supplemental and accident coverage

Accident, critical illness, and hospital indemnity plans pay cash benefits directly to you when an injury keeps you off the road. These pair with your main plan to replace lost income during downtime.

How Much Does Health Insurance Cost for a Truck Driver?

Cost depends on your age, income, state, and family size. Many owner-operators qualify for ACA subsidies that bring premiums down substantially, sometimes to $0 to $80 per month. Drivers who earn above the subsidy threshold typically pay $300 to $600 per month for a strong PPO, which may be partially or fully deductible if you are self-employed. The Owner-Operator Tax Advantage Most Drivers Miss

If you are self-employed and pay your own premiums, you may be able to deduct 100 percent of your health insurance costs through the self-employed health insurance deduction. That applies to premiums for you, your spouse, and your dependents. It is one of the largest tax benefits available to owner-operators, so talk to your tax advisor about claiming it.

How to Get Covered, Step by Step

  1. Know your status. Company driver with a plan, company driver without one, or owner-operator.
  2. Check your subsidy. Self-employed drivers often qualify for tax credits that lower the premium.
  3. Pick a plan built for the road. Prioritize a PPO with a nationwide network.
  4. Add income protection if you want it. Supplemental accident coverage replaces lost income during downtime.
  5. Enroll and hit the road. A licensed broker handles the paperwork so you can keep driving.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do truck drivers get health insurance?

Yes. Company drivers may get coverage through their carrier, while owner-operators and 1099 drivers buy their own plan through the ACA marketplace or a private PPO. Many self-employed drivers qualify for subsidies that lower the cost.

What is the best health insurance for truck drivers?

For long-haul and OTR drivers, a PPO plan with a nationwide network is usually best because you can see any provider in any state without a referral. If your income qualifies for ACA subsidies, an ACA PPO combines broad access with a lower premium.

How much does health insurance cost for a truck driver?

It varies by income, state, age, and family size. Many owner-operators pay $0 to $80 per month after ACA tax credits. Drivers above the subsidy threshold typically pay $300 to $600 per month for a strong PPO, which may be tax deductible for the self-employed.

Can 1099 and self-employed truck drivers get health insurance?

Yes. If you receive a 1099 as an owner-operator or independent contractor, you are self-employed and eligible for ACA marketplace plans and private PPO plans. Many 1099 drivers qualify for premium tax credits.
