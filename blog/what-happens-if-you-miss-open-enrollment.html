What Happens If You Miss Open Enrollment for Health Insurance?

If you missed the open enrollment window, do not panic. It does not mean you are stuck without coverage for the year. There are several ways to still get insured, and this guide walks through exactly what happens next and what to do.

The short version
If you miss open enrollment, you generally cannot buy a standard marketplace plan until the next window, unless you qualify for a special enrollment period. A qualifying life event such as losing coverage, moving, marriage, or a new baby reopens enrollment. Medicaid and other options are available year round.

What Open Enrollment Is

Open enrollment is the yearly window when anyone can sign up for or change a health insurance plan without needing a special reason. For most people buying their own coverage, it runs in the late fall for coverage that starts January 1. Outside that window, enrollment is normally closed, which is why missing it feels like a problem.

What Happens If You Miss It

If you miss open enrollment and nothing else changes in your life, you generally cannot enroll in a standard marketplace plan until the next open enrollment period. That is the default rule. But there are important exceptions, and most people qualify for at least one path to coverage sooner than they think. If you want the exact upcoming dates, see our guide to open enrollment 2027 dates and deadlines. And for the complete list of events that let you enroll right now, see our guide to qualifying life events for health insurance.

Special Enrollment Periods and Qualifying Life Events

A special enrollment period lets you enroll outside the normal window if you have a qualifying life event. These are more common than people realize.

  • Losing existing coverage, such as leaving a job or aging off a parent plan
  • Getting married or divorced
  • Having a baby or adopting a child
  • Moving to a new area or state
  • A change in income that affects your eligibility

Most qualifying events give you a window of about 60 days to enroll, so acting quickly matters.

Options Available Year Round

Even without a qualifying event, some coverage is available any time of year.

  • Medicaid and CHIP. If your income qualifies, you can enroll at any time, not just during open enrollment.
  • Dental and vision plans. These are sold year round and are separate from medical open enrollment.
  • Other private options. Depending on your state and situation, there may be private plans available outside the standard window. These vary in what they cover, so it is important to understand the details before enrolling.

What to Do Right Now

The smartest move is to find out quickly whether you qualify for a special enrollment period, because the clock may already be running. Here is the simple path.

  1. List any life changes in the last 60 days, such as a job change, move, or new family member.
  2. Check whether your income qualifies you for Medicaid, which enrolls year round.
  3. Talk to a licensed advisor who can confirm your options in minutes and get you enrolled if you qualify.

You may have more options than you expect, but many of them are time sensitive, so it is worth checking today.

Missed Open Enrollment FAQ

Can I still get health insurance if I missed open enrollment?

Often yes. If you have a qualifying life event such as losing coverage, moving, marriage, or a new baby, you can enroll during a special enrollment period. If your income qualifies, Medicaid enrolls year round. A licensed advisor can confirm your options quickly.

What counts as a qualifying life event?

Common qualifying events include losing existing coverage, getting married or divorced, having or adopting a child, moving to a new area, and certain income changes. Most events give you about 60 days to enroll.

How long is a special enrollment period?

Most special enrollment periods last about 60 days from the date of your qualifying life event, so it is important to act quickly once something changes.

What if I do not have a qualifying event?

If you do not qualify for a special enrollment period, you generally wait until the next open enrollment. In the meantime, you may still qualify for Medicaid if your income is low enough, and dental and vision plans are available year round.

Is there a penalty for not having health insurance?

There is no federal penalty for being uninsured, though a few states have their own requirements. The bigger risk is financial, since one accident or illness without coverage can be very expensive.

How do I enroll now?

The fastest way is to speak with a licensed advisor who can check your eligibility for a special enrollment period or Medicaid and handle the enrollment for you at no cost.
