Telehealth for Truck Drivers: How to See a Doctor From the Cab

A sinus infection in Amarillo should not cost you a day of driving and an out-of-network urgent care bill. Here is how drivers use virtual visits, and where they stop working.

What a virtual visit is good for

Telehealth means seeing a licensed doctor, nurse practitioner or therapist by video or phone. For a driver, it is often the fastest way to get care without finding parking near a clinic. Good uses:

  • Colds, flu, sinus infections, sore throat, pink eye, allergies
  • Urinary tract infections and minor skin rashes
  • Refills of regular medications and follow-ups on blood pressure, cholesterol or diabetes
  • Reviewing lab results your home doctor ordered
  • Counseling and mental health care, including on a regular schedule from the road

What it cannot do

  • Your DOT physical. The medical exam for your CDL medical card must be done in person by a certified examiner listed on the FMCSA National Registry. A video visit does not count. DOT physical requirements.
  • Emergencies. Chest pain, trouble breathing, signs of a stroke, a serious injury or a bad fall go to the nearest ER. ACA plans must cover emergency care at in-network cost-sharing, even out of state. Using your insurance out of state.
  • Anything that needs hands or a machine: stitches, X-rays, a strep test, a sprain that needs imaging.

The rule most drivers don't know: state licensing

A provider generally has to be licensed in the state where you are physically located during the visit, not where you live. Your hometown doctor may not be able to see you by video while you are parked in Ohio. The national telehealth services that come with many health plans handle this by matching you with a provider licensed in whatever state you are in. That is why, for drivers, the plan's built-in telehealth service is usually more useful than a local doctor's video portal.

What it costs

  • Many Marketplace and employer plans include virtual visits at a low or $0 copay, often with a national telehealth vendor. Check your plan's summary of benefits for "telehealth" or "virtual visits."
  • HSA plans (including every bronze plan since 2026) are permanently allowed to cover telehealth before you meet the deductible, if the plan chooses to. How an HSA works for drivers.
  • Without coverage, cash-pay telehealth services commonly charge a flat fee per visit. It is still usually cheaper than an urgent care visit, but it does not count toward a deductible.

Getting the prescription filled on your route

Ask the provider to send the prescription to a national chain pharmacy near where you will be in the next few hours, not near home. Most chains can transfer it to another location if your route changes. Know your plan's preferred pharmacies and whether it has mail order for regular medications. Filling prescriptions on the road.

Tips for a visit from the cab

  • Book it for your 10-hour break and park somewhere quiet with signal. Truck stop Wi-Fi works for most video visits.
  • Keep a note on your phone with your medications, doses, allergies and your doctor's name.
  • Have your insurance card photo handy, plus your home address for the record.
  • Never do a visit while driving. Many services will end the call if they see you are moving.
  • Ask for a work note if you need one for dispatch.

If you are struggling and need to talk to someone now, call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, from anywhere in the US.

Frequently asked

Can I get my DOT physical through telehealth?

No. The DOT medical exam for a CDL medical card must be done in person by a certified medical examiner listed on the FMCSA National Registry. Telehealth can help you manage conditions like high blood pressure before the exam, but it cannot replace it.

Can my home doctor see me by video when I'm in another state?

Often not. Providers generally must be licensed in the state where you are physically located during the visit. Plan-provided national telehealth services match you with a provider licensed in the state you are in, which is why they tend to work better for drivers.

Does health insurance cover telehealth for truck drivers?

Most Marketplace and employer plans cover virtual visits, often at a low or $0 copay through a national telehealth service. HSA-eligible plans, including every bronze Marketplace plan since 2026, are allowed to cover telehealth before the deductible. Check your plan's summary of benefits.

Can a telehealth doctor send a prescription to a pharmacy on my route?

Yes. Ask the provider to send it to a chain pharmacy near where you will be in the next few hours. Most chains can transfer it to another store if your route changes.
