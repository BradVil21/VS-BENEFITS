Is Dental and Vision Insurance Worth It? Here Is the 2026 Math

Nobody wants another monthly bill, so this is a fair question. The honest answer depends on one thing: whether you actually use dental and vision care. Below is the real math, with 2026 prices for care with and without coverage, so you can decide in five minutes instead of guessing.

💡
The short version
If you see a dentist at least once a year or wear glasses or contacts, coverage usually pays for itself. Preventive dental visits alone often cost more in cash than a year of premiums. If you never use care at all, it may not. The table below shows the break-even point.

What Dental and Vision Insurance Costs in 2026

Prices vary by state, age, and plan level, but here is the realistic range most people see.

What Care Costs Without Insurance

This is the side of the ledger people skip. Cash prices for routine care add up faster than most expect.

ServiceTypical cash price
Cleaning, exam, and X-rays$200 to $400 per visit
Filling$150 to $450
Crown$1,000 to $2,000
Root canal$700 to $1,800
Eye exam$100 to $200
Glasses (frames and lenses)$200 to $600

Two cleanings and one eye exam with new glasses can easily total $700 to $1,400 in cash. A bundled plan covering all of it often costs less than half that for the year, with preventive dental typically covered at 100 percent.

When Coverage Is Clearly Worth It

  • You go to the dentist at least once a year. Preventive visits alone usually beat the premium.
  • You wear glasses or contacts. Annual exams plus new lenses make vision coverage pay for itself almost automatically.
  • You have kids. Children reliably need cleanings, and sooner or later somebody needs braces or glasses.
  • You are self-employed or your job offers no benefits. Nobody is subsidizing your care, so an individual bundle is how you get group-style pricing on your own. Our individual dental and vision plans page covers exactly how that works.
  • You have been putting off dental work. A plan with good major coverage turns a $1,500 crown into a few hundred dollars, once any waiting period passes.

When It May Not Be Worth It

Honesty builds trust, so here it is. If you have not seen a dentist in years, have perfect vision, and would simply pay cash in a rare emergency, premiums may outrun what you use. One warning though: waiting periods on major work mean you cannot buy a plan the week you need a crown and expect it covered. Coverage works when you get it before you need it.

Dental and Vision Insurance FAQ

Is dental and vision insurance worth it for adults?

For most adults who see a dentist even once a year, yes. Two cleanings, exams, and X-rays typically cost more out of pocket than a year of premiums, and preventive care is usually covered at 100 percent. The math gets even stronger if you wear glasses or contacts, or expect any fillings or major work.

How much does dental and vision insurance cost per month?

Standalone dental plans often run between $20 and $50 per month for an individual, vision plans between $10 and $20, and bundled plans commonly land between $25 and $60 depending on your state, age, and coverage level. We can show you exact prices for your zip code for free.

When is dental insurance not worth it?

If you never visit the dentist, have no glasses or contacts, and would pay cash for a rare emergency, a plan may not pay for itself. Waiting periods on major work also mean buying coverage the week you need a crown rarely works.

Is it cheaper to bundle dental and vision together?

Usually yes. Bundled plans typically cost less than two separate policies, come with one bill, and are simpler at claim time. We compare bundled and standalone pricing side by side so you can see which wins.

Does regular health insurance cover dental and vision for adults?

Usually not. Most adult health plans exclude routine dental and vision care, which is why standalone and bundled plans exist. Adults almost always need separate coverage, and our full explainer covers the exceptions.
