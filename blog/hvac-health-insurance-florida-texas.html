Health Insurance for HVAC Companies in Florida and Texas (2027)

Florida and Texas run on air conditioning, and both states are short on the techs who keep it running. If you own an HVAC company in either state, health insurance is one of the strongest tools you have to hire and keep good people. Here is how the rules work in each state, what coverage costs in 2027, and the options that fit an HVAC crew.

The short answer
HVAC companies in Florida and Texas can offer a small group plan (about $350 to $650 per employee per month for employee-only coverage), an ICHRA, or help techs buy their own plans. The key difference: Florida treats businesses with 1 to 50 employees, including the self-employed, as small employers, while Texas starts at 2 employees. Neither state requires a company under 50 full-time equivalents to offer coverage. Get group quotes for your crew.

Why benefits matter more for HVAC in Florida and Texas

No state employs more HVAC mechanics and installers than Florida: the Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 37,370 in May 2023. Texas had 31,910, third in the country. Cooling demand runs most of the year in both states, so every shop is competing for the same licensed techs.

Both states also have a lot of people without health insurance. In 2024, 16.7% of Texans were uninsured, the highest rate in the country, and Florida stood at 10.9%, compared with 8.2% nationally. Many of the techs you interview have never had employer coverage. A shop that offers it stands out right away.

Health insurance for HVAC companies in Florida

Florida law defines a small employer as a business with at least 1 and no more than 50 employees, and it expressly includes self-employed individuals. That means even a very small HVAC company can shop small group coverage in Florida. Carriers still set their own participation and contribution rules, so it pays to compare more than one.

Florida employers are not required to offer coverage until they reach 50 full-time equivalent employees. For techs buying their own plans, Florida insurers asked for an average increase of about 15.3% for 2027, and regulators approved a lower weighted average of about 10.4%, after a 31.5% average increase in 2026. Health insurance for HVAC companies in Texas

The Texas Insurance Code defines a small employer as one that employed an average of at least 2 and no more than 50 employees during the prior year and has at least 2 employees on the first day of the plan year. A one-person HVAC business in Texas generally can’t buy small group coverage, so the owner buys an individual plan until the first hire.

The Texas Department of Insurance notes that state law doesn’t require you to pay your employees’ premiums, but your insurance company may require you to pay at least 50%. Texas individual-market insurers asked for an average increase of about 14.1% for 2027, after a 34.7% average increase in 2026.

1099 subs and self-employed HVAC contractors

A genuine 1099 subcontractor can’t join your group plan. Self-employed HVAC contractors in either state buy individual coverage, and Marketplace tax credits are based on net income after the truck, tools and other business costs. A single person qualifies up to about $63,840 for 2027 coverage. See health insurance for HVAC technicians.

If you control a sub’s hours, tools and methods, they may be an employee in practice, which changes both your headcount and your obligations. 1099 crews vs. W-2 employees.

  1. List each W-2 employee’s age, ZIP code and average weekly hours.
  2. Decide who’s eligible, for example everyone at 30+ hours, after a waiting period of up to 90 days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do HVAC companies in Florida or Texas have to offer health insurance?

Not until they have 50 or more full-time equivalent employees. Neither state adds its own employer mandate. At 50 or more, the 2027 federal penalty for offering no coverage is $3,780 per full-time employee, minus the first 30.

How much does group health insurance cost for an HVAC company in Florida or Texas?

Small group coverage usually runs about $350 to $650 per employee per month for employee-only coverage, before the employer's share. Rates depend mostly on employee ages, ZIP code and plan design.

Can a one-person HVAC business get group health insurance?

In Florida, state law counts businesses with 1 to 50 employees, including the self-employed, as small employers, so it may be possible depending on the carrier. In Texas, a small employer needs at least 2 employees, so a one-person business buys an individual plan.

Can I cover my W-2 techs and not my 1099 subcontractors?

Yes. Genuine 1099 subcontractors can't join a group plan. They buy individual coverage, often with Marketplace tax credits based on their net income.

What if my HVAC crew grows every summer?

Seasonal workers who aren't expected to work 30+ hours for a full measurement period usually don't have to be offered coverage. An ICHRA can also give you a fixed cost per tech as headcount changes.
