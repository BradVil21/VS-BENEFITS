How Many Employees Do You Need for Group Health Insurance in Florida?

Most Florida owners assume they are too small to offer a group plan. Under Florida statute 627.6699, a “small employer” is any business with at least 1 and no more than 50 eligible employees. Two enrolled employees gets you guaranteed-issue coverage. One is possible with caveats. Here is the whole picture, including the two headcount thresholds that quietly change your obligations.

What Florida Law Actually Says

Florida’s Employee Health Care Access Act — statute 627.6699 — defines a small employer as a business that "employed an average of at least 1 but not more than 50 eligible employees" in the preceding calendar year and "employs at least 1 employee on the first day of the plan year."

The statute also requires every small employer carrier to "offer and issue all small employer health benefit plans on a guaranteed-issue basis to every eligible small employer, with 2 to 50 eligible employees." Guaranteed issue means the carrier cannot decline you or rate you up based on health status or claims history. That protection kicks in at two.

The headline

Two enrolled employees — and the owner can be one of them — is enough for a guaranteed-issue group health plan in Florida. If you have a business partner, a spouse on payroll, or a single full-time hire, you likely already qualify.

The Practical Answer: Two

For nearly every Florida small business, two is the number that matters. At two enrolled eligible employees:

  • Every major carrier writing in Florida must issue you a plan — guaranteed issue, no medical underwriting on a fully insured small-group plan
  • You get the full menu of Bronze through Platinum plans, HMO and PPO
  • You can start coverage any month of the year — group plans are not restricted to the November 1 to January 15 Open Enrollment window that applies to individual ACA coverage
  • Premiums the business pays are generally deductible as a business expense
  • You are on the ladder for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit if you meet the wage and contribution tests

A two-person operation in Hialeah and a fifty-person operation in Plantation are governed by the same rules. The pricing differs; the access does not.

Groups of One: Possible, But Read the Fine Print

Florida’s definition of a small employer starts at one, so a single-employee group is contemplated by the statute — but it does not carry the same guaranteed-issue protection, and the statute permits carriers to treat it differently:

  • Rates can be up to 150% of the standard 2–50 group rate. The statute caps the premium for a one-employee group at "150 percent of the rate determined for small employer groups of 2-50 eligible employees."
  • Preexisting condition exclusions can run up to 24 months if the employee had a gap in creditable coverage of more than 63 days before the effective date.
  • Late enrollees can be excluded for up to 24 months.
  • Carrier participation varies. Not every carrier writes one-life groups, and those that do often restrict when they will accept them.

In practice, most true solo owners in Florida do better on an ACA marketplace plan with a subsidy review than on a marked-up one-life group plan. That is not always true — it depends on your income, your household, and whether you need a specific network — but it is the way to bet, and we run both numbers before recommending either.

If your business runs on 1099 labor — common in South Florida trucking, construction and delivery — you can still cover a W-2 core on a group plan and set up ACA coverage for the contractors separately. It is a routine arrangement and we build it often.

The Participation Rules That Actually Trip People Up

Meeting the headcount is only half of it. Florida carriers also enforce a minimum participation requirement — typically 50% to 75% of eligible employees must enroll, varying by carrier and group size. Some carriers require 100% participation for groups of one to three.

The detail that saves groups: employees with other creditable coverage — a spouse’s employer plan, Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, VA — can generally be waived out of the denominator. So a 10-person shop where 4 people are on a spouse’s plan only needs enrollment from a majority of the remaining 6, not of all 10.

Groups fail participation and get declined over this more often than over anything else, and it is almost always avoidable with a properly documented waiver form collected up front.

At 25 Employees: The Tax Credit Line

Cross 25 full-time equivalents and you lose access to the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit, which is worth up to 50% of employer premium contributions (35% for tax-exempt organizations). To qualify, per the IRS, you need fewer than 25 FTEs, average annual wages below an inflation-adjusted threshold, an employer contribution of at least 50% of employee-only premium, and coverage offered through the SHOP Marketplace, with limited exceptions. It is claimable for two consecutive tax years.

If you are at 22 or 23 employees and planning to hire, this is worth modeling before you do. The credit does not vanish at exactly 25 — it phases down as you approach it — but the difference between 24 and 26 employees can be meaningful money.

At 50 Full-Time Equivalents: The Employer Mandate

Cross 50 FTEs and you become an Applicable Large Employer under the ACA. That means you are required to offer affordable, minimum-value coverage to full-time employees or face penalties, and you take on IRS Forms 1094-C and 1095-C reporting obligations.

FTE math is not headcount. Part-time hours aggregate: two employees at 15 hours a week count as roughly one FTE. Seasonal spikes matter too. A Hollywood restaurant group or a Sunrise retail operation with heavy seasonal staffing can cross 50 FTEs without ever having 50 people on the schedule at once.

What to Do Next

  1. Count your W-2 employees, including yourself. Two or more? You qualify for guaranteed-issue group coverage in Florida today.
  2. Collect waiver documentation from anyone covered elsewhere, before you apply. This is what protects your participation rate.
  3. Run your FTE count if you are above 20 — it determines both tax credit eligibility and mandate exposure.
  4. Get a real quote. Ranges are for planning; your census produces the actual number, usually within a business day.
