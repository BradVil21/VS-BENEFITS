Turning 26? Here Is Exactly How to Get Your Own Health Insurance

The ACA lets you stay on a parent's health plan until you turn 26. Then you are on your own, and the clock is stricter than most people realize. Here is when your coverage actually ends, the 60 day window that protects you, what coverage costs at 26, and the mistakes that leave people uninsured.

When Your Coverage Actually Ends

The cutoff is age 26, but the exact date depends on the type of plan your parent has. Most employer plans end your coverage at the end of the month you turn 26. Marketplace plans usually keep you covered through December 31 of the year you turn 26. Do not guess: one call to the plan tells you your real end date, and everything else keys off that.

Turning 26 Is a Qualifying Life Event

Losing your parent's coverage counts as a loss of coverage, which opens a Special Enrollment Period. That means you can enroll in your own plan year round, no open enrollment needed. You generally get 60 days before your coverage ends and 60 days after. Use the before window and your new plan starts the day after the old one ends, with zero gap. The full rules live in our guide to qualifying life events.

The mistake that costs people months
The 60 day window closes whether you used it or not. Miss it and you may be waiting for November open enrollment with no coverage in between. If your birthday is coming up, start two months early.

Your Options at 26

  • A job with benefits. If your employer offers coverage, compare it first. The employer usually pays part of the premium.
  • A marketplace plan with subsidies. Subsidies are based on your income, not your parents'. Early career income often qualifies for meaningful help, which makes this the best value for many 26 year olds.
  • Catastrophic plans. Under 30, you can buy a low premium, high deductible plan that mainly protects against worst case bills. Cheap, but compare it against a subsidized bronze plan before choosing, because the subsidy often makes the better plan cost less.
  • Medicaid. If your income is low or you are between jobs, you may qualify year round in most states.

What It Costs at 26

Premiums are age rated, and 26 is near the bottom of the curve. This is the cheapest decade of your life to be insured. With a subsidy, many young adults pay far less than they expect, sometimes very little at all. The number that matters is your quote, not the national average, and it takes five minutes to get. The Three Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Assuming you have until your birthday. Employer plans often end you at the end of your birthday month, and the paperwork should start well before that.
  2. Buying the cheapest plan without checking subsidies. A subsidized silver plan often beats a catastrophic plan on price and coverage at once.
  3. Going without and hoping. One ER visit costs more than years of premiums. The 60 day window exists so you never have to gamble.

Turning 26 FAQ

When exactly do I lose my parents health insurance at 26?

Most employer plans end your coverage the month you turn 26. Marketplace plans typically cover you through December 31 of the year you turn 26. Check with the plan directly so you know your real deadline.

Is turning 26 a qualifying life event?

Yes. Aging off a parent plan is a loss of coverage, which opens a Special Enrollment Period with a 60 day window on each side of the loss.

How much does health insurance cost at 26?

Age 26 is one of the cheapest times to buy coverage, and many young adults qualify for income based subsidies that cut the price further. A free quote shows your actual number in minutes.

Can I stay on my parents plan past 26?

Generally no. A few states extend dependent coverage for specific situations and disabled dependents may remain covered, but for most people 26 is the cutoff.

What if I missed my 60 day window?

You may need to wait for open enrollment, but check whether you qualify for Medicaid or another qualifying event first. We review it for free.
