Health Insurance for Real Estate Agents: Coverage Options and the Tax Write-Off

Real estate is one of the best careers for freedom and income, but it comes with a catch: your brokerage does not give you health insurance. The good news is that as a self-employed agent you have strong options, including a tax deduction that W-2 employees never get. Here is how to get covered affordably on commission income and write your premiums off.

The short version
As a self-employed real estate agent you buy your own plan, you may deduct 100% of your premiums, and subsidies based on your net income can cut your cost..

Why Real Estate Agents Need Their Own Coverage

Almost every real estate agent is an independent contractor. Your brokerage hands you a 1099, not a benefits package, which means health insurance is on you. Skipping it is a gamble that one accident or illness can turn into a five-figure bill, and the right plan costs far less than most agents expect.

Because you control your own coverage, it is also more stable than employer insurance. It does not disappear if you change brokerages, take a slow season, or go fully independent. Your plan stays with you. The team at VS Health Benefits builds, including the ups and downs of commission income.

The Tax Write-Off Most Agents Miss

This is the part that makes coverage cheaper than it looks. Because you are self-employed, you may qualify for the self-employed health insurance deduction, which lets you deduct 100% of your health insurance premiums on your federal return. A W-2 employee cannot do that.

In plain terms, money you were going to spend on coverage anyway can lower your taxable income. The deduction is generally limited to your net self-employment profit and has a few rules, so your accountant should confirm the exact number for your situation. We are a licensed insurance brokerage, not a tax advisor, but we will gladly coordinate with your CPA.

Why the deduction matters for agents

  • It lowers your taxable income. Premiums you pay for yourself, your spouse, and dependents can usually be deducted.
  • It stacks with subsidies. If you get an ACA subsidy, you reduce your premium up front and may still deduct what you pay.
  • It rewards being covered. Going without insurance gives you no deduction and full exposure to a big bill.

Your Coverage Options

You have the same individual market options as anyone buying their own plan, and we compare all of them for free.

  • ACA marketplace plans. Often the most affordable route because of income-based subsidies. Great when your net income lands in the subsidy range.
  • Private PPO plans. Broad networks and no referrals needed. A strong fit if your income is above subsidy limits or you want maximum flexibility and provider choice.
  • Dental and vision. Inexpensive add-ons that round out your coverage and are also deductible as a self-employed agent.

Subsidies and Commission Income

Commission income is uneven, and that actually works in your favor for subsidies. ACA premium tax credits are based on your estimated net income for the year, not a fixed salary. After your business deductions, many agents land in a range that qualifies for meaningful subsidies.

The key is setting an accurate income estimate up front so you get the right credit and avoid a surprise at tax time. We help you set that estimate and you can update it anytime your year changes. Get a free quote and we will check your subsidy before comparing plans.

How to Choose the Right Plan

A simple way to decide

  • Estimate your net income first. It drives your subsidy and the size of your deduction.
  • Match the network to your doctors. Make sure your preferred providers are covered before you enroll.
  • Balance premium vs deductible. A low premium with a high deductible can cost more if you use care often.
  • Let an advisor compare for you. We pull ACA and private options side by side so you see the real trade-offs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is health insurance tax deductible for real estate agents?

Most agents are self-employed and may deduct 100% of their premiums through the self-employed health insurance deduction. It is limited to your net self-employment income and has rules, so confirm with your accountant. VS Health Benefits is not a tax advisor.

How do agents get coverage without an employer?

You buy your own plan on the individual market: ACA marketplace, private PPO, or other options. We compare them all for free and check your subsidy.

Can agents qualify for subsidies?

Yes. Subsidies are based on estimated net income, so many agents with commission income qualify for premium tax credits that lower their cost.

What is the best plan for a real estate agent?

It depends on your income, health needs, and budget. ACA with subsidies is often most affordable, while private PPO plans offer broader networks. We compare both for free.
