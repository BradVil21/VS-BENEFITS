Other Ways to Get Health Insurance Besides the Marketplace

Most people think health insurance comes from two places: a job or HealthCare.gov. There are actually four kinds of plans, and two of them are sold outside the Marketplace, some of them any time of year. If Marketplace prices are too high, you missed Open Enrollment, or you just want to see what else is out there, here is every option side by side.

The short answer
There are four ways to get health insurance: ACA Marketplace plans and employer plans (you can’t be turned down), and two private-market options: medically underwritten plans, which ask health questions but can cost less for healthy people and can be bought year-round, and short-term, discount or MEC plans, which are cheaper but limited. A licensed broker can quote all four.

The 4 types of health insurance plans

Every plan falls into one of four groups. The left side is the private market, where insurers can look at your health. The right side is guaranteed issue: you can’t be turned down or charged more for a health condition.

1. ACA Marketplace plans (Obamacare)

These are the plans on HealthCare.gov and state exchanges. Every one covers the essential health benefits, and insurers can’t deny you or charge more for a pre-existing condition.

  • Tax credits lower the premium based on your income. Check yours with our 2027 subsidy calculator.
  • When to enroll: Open Enrollment runs November 1 to January 15 in most states, or within 60 days of a qualifying life event.
  • The catch: if your income is above the subsidy cliff, you pay full price, and full price went up again for 2027.

2. Employer (group) plans

Coverage through a job, or through your own business if you have employees. Eligible employees can’t be turned down for health reasons, and the employer pays part of the premium.

  • If your employer offers affordable coverage, you usually can’t get Marketplace tax credits instead.
  • Own a business? Options include small group plans, level-funded plans and an ICHRA. See small business health insurance.
  • A one-person business, or a husband-and-wife business with no other employees, usually needs individual coverage instead.

3. Medically underwritten private plans

This is the option most people have never heard of. These are private plans, often PPOs with national networks, sold outside the Marketplace. You answer health questions, and the insurer decides whether to offer coverage and at what price.

  • Who it fits: healthy people, especially those earning too much for a tax credit, who would otherwise pay full Marketplace price.
  • You can apply any time of year, not just during Open Enrollment.
  • The catch: you can be declined, and some conditions can be limited or excluded. Read exactly what is covered before you switch.

More: private health insurance · benefits of private health insurance

4. Short-term, discount and MEC plans

These are the lowest-cost options, and the most limited. They are not ACA coverage.

  • Short-term plans cover some unexpected medical costs for a limited time and can exclude pre-existing conditions. How long one can last depends on your state, and the federal rules on their length are changing.
  • Discount plans are not insurance. They give you lower prices at participating providers, and you still pay the bill.
  • MEC (minimum essential coverage) plans mostly cover preventive care, such as checkups and screenings.

They can work as a bridge between jobs or until your next enrollment window. They are risky as your only coverage. See short-term health insurance and COBRA alternatives.

Which option is right for you?

  • You have a health condition or take ongoing prescriptions: start with ACA or employer coverage, where you can’t be turned down.
  • You’re healthy and earn too much for a tax credit: compare a medically underwritten plan against full Marketplace price. The difference can be large.
  • You missed Open Enrollment: look at medically underwritten plans, which you can apply for year-round, or a short-term plan as a bridge.
  • You just lost a job: you have a special enrollment period for ACA plans. Compare it with COBRA before you pay COBRA’s price.
  • You own a business with employees: compare group plans, level-funded plans and an ICHRA.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the other ways to get health insurance besides the Marketplace?

Besides ACA Marketplace plans, you can get coverage through an employer (group) plan, a medically underwritten private plan sold outside the Marketplace, or a limited plan such as a short-term, discount or MEC plan.

Can I buy health insurance outside of Open Enrollment?

Yes. Medically underwritten private plans and short-term plans can usually be bought any time of year. ACA Marketplace plans require Open Enrollment or a qualifying life event, such as losing other coverage.

Is private health insurance cheaper than Obamacare?

It can be for healthy people who don't qualify for a Marketplace tax credit. If you qualify for a tax credit or have health conditions, an ACA plan is often the better deal. Compare both before you choose.

Can a private health plan deny me?

Medically underwritten plans can decline coverage or limit some conditions based on your health answers. ACA and employer plans can't deny you for a health condition.

Are short-term health plans real insurance?

They are insurance, but limited. They don't have to cover pre-existing conditions or all essential health benefits, and they only last for a limited time. Discount plans are not insurance at all.

Does it cost more to use a broker?

No. Licensed brokers are paid by the insurance companies, so there is no charge to you, and a broker can compare options across all four types.
