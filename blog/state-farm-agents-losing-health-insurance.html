State Farm Agents Are Losing Their Health Insurance: What to Do Now

What State Farm changed

In May 2026 State Farm told agents it was replacing its several agent contract types with one new agreement built around digital sales and new-business incentives. Along with lower base compensation and the end of the Annual Investment Payment Program (AIPP), agents learned that State Farm would no longer offer health insurance to agents and their spouses. Agents also reported that dental and vision coverage was ending. A May 19 letter to retired agents said their non-contractual group benefits and Medicare supplement support end at the end of 2026.

After strong pushback, State Farm restored AIPP payments for 2026 through 2028 in a June 12 message to agents. The health insurance change stayed. Agents who do not sign the new agreement can apply for a transition program instead.

Whatever you decide about the contract, the health insurance question is the same: you are a self-employed business owner who now buys your own coverage.

Who is affected

  • Agents and spouses covered through State Farm's agent health plan. If your children are on the plan, confirm in your notice how their coverage ends.
  • Retired agents who received Medicare supplement support. See retired agents below.
  • Your agency team, indirectly. Your team members are your employees, not State Farm's. If you were planning to rely on State Farm benefits for your own family, this is a good moment to decide whether a group plan for the whole agency makes more sense.

State Farm agents are independent contractors, so the options are the same as for any self-employed business owner. Which one wins depends mostly on your household income, your health and whether you have employees.

Will you qualify for a subsidy?

Premium tax credits are based on your household's projected 2027 modified adjusted gross income. For an agent, that starts with net profit from the agency after business expenses, not gross commissions. With the enhanced credits expired, there is no credit above 400% of the federal poverty level: roughly $63,000 for a single person and about $130,000 for a family of four.

Two things make this worth a careful estimate this year. Lower base compensation and the shift to new-business incentives can make 2027 income harder to predict. And retirement contributions such as a SEP IRA or Solo 401(k), plus HSA contributions, can lower the income that counts. See what income counts for ACA subsidies and the subsidy calculator.

Timing: how to avoid a gap

  1. Find your end date. Read the notice from State Farm and note the last day of coverage for you, your spouse and any children.
  2. Use Open Enrollment if your plan ends December 31. Open Enrollment runs November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15 for a January 1 start.
  3. If it ends at another time, losing qualifying health coverage generally opens a 60-day special enrollment period. You can usually apply up to 60 days before the coverage ends, so the new plan starts the day after.
  4. Private plans don't wait for a window. Underwritten private plans accept applications year-round. Apply early so approval comes before your old plan ends, and keep your current coverage until the new policy is issued.
  5. List your doctors and prescriptions and check them against every plan before you enroll.

The tax side

As self-employed business owners, agents can generally deduct health insurance premiums for themselves, a spouse and dependents under the self-employed health insurance deduction, as long as they are not eligible for an employer plan through a spouse's job and the deduction does not exceed the agency's net profit. If you also get a premium tax credit, the two interact, so plan with your tax professional. More: how the self-employed health insurance deduction works.

Covering your agency team

If you have W-2 team members, you have more choices than an individual agent. A small group plan can cover you, your spouse if employed, and your staff, often with a broader network than individual plans. An ICHRA or QSEHRA lets you give each person a monthly allowance toward their own plan. If it is only you and your spouse, you generally cannot buy a small group plan; see health insurance for a husband-and-wife business.

If you are a retired agent

Reports say the Medicare supplement support for retired agents, about $2,400 a year per person or $4,800 with a spouse, ends at the end of 2026. Losing that support does not cancel your Medicare or Medigap policy by itself; it means more of the premium comes out of your pocket. If you are not yet 65 and lose retiree coverage, see health insurance before Medicare.

Frequently asked

Is State Farm ending health insurance for its agents?

Yes. Reporting since May 2026 says State Farm will no longer offer health insurance to its roughly 19,000 exclusive agents and their spouses as part of a new agent contract. When State Farm reversed part of its deferred compensation change in June 2026, it did not reverse the end of agent health and life insurance support.

When does State Farm agent health insurance end?

Reports differ on the exact date. Local coverage in Bloomington, Illinois said support for agent health and life insurance ends at the end of 2026, while other reports tie the changes to the new contract that agents must sign by 2028. Check the notice State Farm sent you, and plan replacement coverage before that date so there is no gap.

Can State Farm agents get an ACA subsidy?

Possibly. As self-employed independent contractors, agents qualify based on their household's projected 2027 modified adjusted gross income, which for most agents starts with net business profit, not gross commissions. With the enhanced credits expired, there is no credit above 400% of the federal poverty level, roughly $63,000 for a single person and about $130,000 for a family of four. Lower or less predictable commissions can move some agents under that line.

Can I deduct health insurance premiums as a State Farm agent?

Self-employed people can generally deduct health insurance premiums for themselves, a spouse and dependents under the self-employed health insurance deduction, as long as they are not eligible for an employer plan through a spouse's job and the deduction does not exceed business net profit. Confirm with your tax professional.

Can my agency set up a group health plan for me and my team?

Usually, if you have at least one W-2 employee who is not you or your spouse. A small group plan, an ICHRA or a QSEHRA can then cover your team, and a group plan can cover you as the owner too. An agency with only the owner and a spouse generally cannot buy a small group plan and uses individual coverage instead.

What happens to retired State Farm agents' Medicare supplement support?

Reports say the Medicare supplement support for retired agents, about $2,400 a year per person ($4,800 with a spouse), ends at the end of 2026. That support ending does not by itself cancel your Medicare or Medigap policy; it means you pay more of the premium yourself.
