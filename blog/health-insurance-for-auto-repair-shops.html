Health Insurance for Auto Repair Shops: Keep Your Techs Out of the Dealership

The dealership down the road just offered your A-tech a signing bonus — and a health plan. In a market where certified technicians can pick their shop, benefits have become the difference between a bay that produces and a bay you're re-staffing every year. Here's what coverage really costs a shop, who has to be on the plan, and how even a two-person garage qualifies.

The Dealership Problem Every Independent Shop Knows

Dealerships can't always beat your culture, your hours, or your flat-rate math — but they almost always beat independents on benefits. When an ASE-certified tech weighs two offers and one includes health coverage for his family, the wrench usually follows the insurance card. Losing an A-tech costs you months of reduced car count while you recruit, vet, and ramp a replacement — if you can find one at all.

The fix is more attainable than most shop owners assume: in most states, a group health plan starts at two enrolled employees. You and your service writer are a group.

The Coverage Options That Fit a Shop

  • Fully insured group plans. Traditional, predictable, easy to administer. Enroll any month of the year — no open enrollment wait.
  • Level funded plans. Fixed monthly cost with a potential refund if claims stay low. Shop crews tend to be young and healthy — exactly who level funding rewards, often at 10 to 30 percent below fully insured rates.
  • ICHRA. Set a fixed tax-free monthly reimbursement and let each tech pick his own plan. Full budget control for you, full choice for them.
  • Dental, vision, and life add-ons. $8 to $30 a person, and they make an offer letter look complete when you're recruiting against a dealership package.

What It Actually Costs a Shop

Most small-shop group plans land between roughly $350 and $650 per enrolled employee per month for single coverage — split between shop and employee however you set it. Put it in shop math: a $300 monthly contribution toward your lead tech's plan is about $10 a working day — less than the margin on one oil change, against the five-figure cost of replacing him. Shops under 25 employees may also qualify for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit.

Your actual number depends on ages and zip codes — a free quote shows it in about 10 minutes, no health questions needed.

Flat-Rate Techs, Service Writers, and Part-Timers

Eligibility runs on hours, not pay structure. Flat-rate techs count as full-time when they average 30+ hours a week in the shop, same as hourly or salaried staff. Your part-time detailer or weekend counter help under the threshold doesn't have to be on the plan — and often qualifies for a subsidized ACA marketplace plan instead, which we can set up at no cost.

Yes, a 2-Person Shop Qualifies

The most common myth in this trade is that group insurance is for big operations. Carriers write groups starting at two enrolled employees, and level funded plans frequently price small, healthy shop teams better than anything the owner could buy individually. If you've been buying a marketplace plan for yourself at full price, a group of two might genuinely cost less — worth a quote just to check.

How to Get Your Shop Covered

  1. List each employee's age, zip code, and average weekly hours — that's the whole census
  2. Decide eligibility: everyone at 30+ hours, or techs and writers first
  3. Pick your contribution — 50% of the employee-only premium is a common start
  4. Get quotes across every major carrier, including level funded
  5. We handle enrollment, ID cards, and tech questions so you stay under the hood
