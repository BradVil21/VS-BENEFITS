Georgia Has Not Expanded Medicaid, With One Narrow Exception

Georgia is one of the states that has not adopted full Medicaid expansion. It runs a limited program with a work requirement attached, which covers far fewer people than a standard expansion would.

The result is a coverage gap similar to Texas. A large number of working Georgians, particularly self-employed people and those in service and hospitality work, land above traditional Medicaid eligibility and below marketplace subsidy eligibility. For those Georgians, standalone dental and vision is often the only coverage that is realistically available.

Georgia Access: The State Exchange Change

Georgia moved off HealthCare.gov to its own state-based exchange, called Georgia Access. If you have enrolled in coverage in Georgia in the past and are used to the federal site, the process is now different. This trips people up, and some miss enrollment deadlines simply because they went to the wrong place.

Standalone dental and vision can be purchased outside the exchange at any time of year, and is not subject to the exchange's open enrollment window. That is a genuine advantage if you have already missed a deadline.

Costs in Georgia

Standalone adult dental in Georgia typically runs about $20 to $48 per month. Vision is generally $10 to $17. Atlanta cash prices run close to the national average: a cleaning and exam commonly $140 to $230, a filling $160 to $360, a crown $950 to $1,650.

Atlanta Versus the Rest of Georgia

Metro Atlanta has an extremely deep dental network and you will have wide choice at almost any plan level. Outside the metro, Georgia thins out fast. South Georgia and the rural counties have significant provider shortages, and several counties have very few dentists accepting new patients at all.

If you live outside metro Atlanta, this is the single most important thing to check. Pull the actual in-network provider list for your county before you buy, and call one of them to confirm they are accepting new patients. A plan with a large statewide network number is meaningless if nobody within an hour of you is taking patients.

Annual Maximums Matter More When You Have No Medical Coverage

Because Georgia's coverage gap leaves a lot of people without medical insurance, dental problems can escalate into medical emergencies without anything catching them in between. An untreated abscess becomes an ER visit, and an ER visit without medical coverage becomes a very large bill.

If dental is the only coverage you will have, it is worth paying a little more for a higher annual maximum and a shorter waiting period on major work, rather than optimizing for the lowest premium.
