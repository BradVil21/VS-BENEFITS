Driving in Montana: what it means for your coverage

  • I-90
  • I-94
  • I-15

Montana distances are measured in hours, not miles. I-90 and I-94 run east-west, and I-15 heads north to the Canadian crossing at Sweetgrass. In much of the state, the nearest full-service hospital is hours away, so Montana drivers should know which hospitals in Billings, Missoula or Great Falls their plan covers.

Your plan follows your Montana address, not your route. Health plans are sold by state of legal residence, which is usually the address on your CDL and your tax return. You can drive in 48 states and still buy a Montana plan. What changes is how that plan pays when you are somewhere else, and that is the part most drivers never check.

Network reach: the question to ask before price

Two Montana plans can cost about the same and work completely differently on the road. HMO and EPO plans pay only inside their network, which is often Montana-only, so a sinus infection in another state can become your bill. PPO plans also pay outside the network, and some are built on national networks so the doctor at your next stop is in network.

Regional drivers who are home most nights can often save money with a strong local network and rely on urgent care and telehealth on the road. Long-haul drivers usually do better with a PPO. We show you both side by side, with the network named, so you are not guessing.

DOT physical flagged your blood pressure or blood sugar? Don't wait. Every ACA plan covers pre-existing conditions, so a flagged reading can't be used to deny you. Getting covered before your next medical card renewal means you can see a doctor and get treated, instead of hoping the numbers come down on their own.

Remember that tax credits are based on your net income after truck expenses, not the gross on your settlement sheets. A lot of Montana owner-operators qualify for more help than they expect. Not sure what your number is? Try the cost calculator or just ask your advisor.

Montana truck driver health insurance FAQ

Can I buy Montana health insurance if I'm on the road most of the year?

Yes. Your plan is based on your legal residence, which is usually the address on your CDL and your tax return, not the states you drive through. If your home base is in Montana, you buy a Montana plan, even if you spend most nights in the truck.

Will a Montana plan cover me when I'm in another state?

Emergency care is covered anywhere in the country on every ACA plan. Routine care is where plans differ. HMO and EPO plans pay only inside their network, which is often Montana-only. PPO plans pay outside the network too, and some are built on national networks with in-network doctors in all 50 states. We show you which Montana plans travel and which don't.

Can low-income truck drivers in Montana get Medicaid?

Montana expanded Medicaid. If your net income after truck expenses comes in under about 138% of the federal poverty level, which happens in a first year with your own authority, you may qualify for Medicaid. Above that, marketplace tax credits lower your premium.

When can Montana truck drivers enroll?

During open enrollment: November 1 to January 15. Enroll by December 15 if you want coverage to start January 1. Outside that window, losing coverage opens a 60-day special enrollment period. That includes leaving a company job to lease on or run your own authority.
