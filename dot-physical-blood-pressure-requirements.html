DOT physical blood pressure requirements: the limits, your card length, and how to pass

Blood pressure is the number that most often shortens a CDL medical card. It doesn't just decide pass or fail — it decides whether you walk out with a 2-year card, a 1-year card, or 3 months to fix it.

Quick answer: the DOT blood pressure limit is 140/90. At or under 140/90 you can get a card for up to 2 years. Higher readings get a shorter card, and 180/110 or above disqualifies you until it comes down.

The DOT blood pressure chart

Certified medical examiners follow FMCSA's hypertension guidance. The top number (systolic) or bottom number (diastolic), whichever is in the higher stage, decides where you land:

140/90 or lowerNormal / controlledUp to 2 yearsAt least yearly if you're being treated for high blood pressure

Source: FMCSA guidance on certification by hypertension stage. Your examiner makes the final call and can certify for less time if something else needs monitoring.

Stage 2: how to turn a 3-month card into a 1-year card

A 3-month card is a warning, not the end of your career. It's a one-time certificate, so the clock matters:

  1. See a doctor this week. Telehealth works from the truck. Bring the examiner's reading.
  2. Start or adjust treatment. Most blood pressure medicines are inexpensive generics.
  3. Track it. A home cuff and a log of readings shows the examiner it's controlled.
  4. Go back before the 3 months run out. Under 140/90 gets you a 1-year card dated from your original exam.

Drivers who miss the window can't get a second 3-month card. That's where having a doctor already lined up — and a plan that pays for the visit — makes the difference.

Stage 3: the road back

At 180/110 or higher you can't be certified that day. Once treatment brings you under 140/90, you can be certified again, but on a 6-month cycle instead of 1 or 2 years. That means more exams, more fees and more time off the road until things stay stable.

Already on blood pressure medication?

Taking blood pressure medicine does not disqualify you. Drivers being treated for hypertension should expect a card of 1 year at most, even when their reading is under 140/90. The examiner will also ask about side effects — dizziness or fatigue that could affect driving — so tell your doctor what you do for a living when they choose your medicine.

How to get an honest reading on exam day

A lot of drivers fail on a reading that isn't their real number. A few things push blood pressure up in the moment:

  • Caffeine, nicotine and energy drinks in the hours before the exam.
  • Decongestants in many cold and sinus medicines.
  • Rushing in late after a long drive or a fight with traffic.
  • White-coat nerves. If your first reading is high, it's reasonable to ask whether it can be rechecked after you've sat quietly for a few minutes.

Take your regular medicine as prescribed — don't skip it to "test clean," and don't change anything without asking your doctor.

The real fix: a doctor between physicals

The DOT physical usually isn't covered by health insurance (here's why). But everything that keeps you under 140/90 is: primary care visits, generic blood pressure medicine, lab work and follow-ups. A driver who sees a doctor once or twice a year keeps 2-year cards. A driver with no coverage finds out at the exam.

If you're an owner-operator, your ACA subsidy is based on net income after expenses, not gross settlements — many drivers who think they earn too much actually qualify. See health insurance for owner-operators or check the real cost for truck drivers.

DOT blood pressure questions

What is the blood pressure limit for a DOT physical?

140/90. At or below 140/90 a driver can be certified for up to 2 years (drivers being treated for high blood pressure are certified at least once a year). Above that, the reading sets a shorter card: 140–159/90–99 gets 1 year, 160–179/100–109 gets a one-time 3-month card, and 180/110 or higher is disqualifying until it comes down.

Can I pass a DOT physical with high blood pressure?

Usually, yes. Stage 1 (140–159/90–99) still gets a 1-year card. Stage 2 (160–179/100–109) gets a one-time 3-month card, and if you come back under 140/90 within those 3 months you get a 1-year card from the date of your original exam. Only stage 3 (180/110 or higher) disqualifies, and even then you can be certified again every 6 months once you are under 140/90.

Can I take blood pressure medication and still drive a truck?

Yes. Taking blood pressure medication does not disqualify you. If you are on treatment for hypertension, expect to be certified at least once a year, and the examiner will ask whether the medicine causes side effects like dizziness that could affect driving.

What happens if I get a 3-month DOT card?

A 3-month card means your reading was stage 2 (160–179/100–109). It is a one-time certificate. If you see a doctor, start or adjust treatment, and return under 140/90 within the 3 months, you can be certified for 1 year from the date of the original exam. If you don't get it down in time, you can't be recertified on another 3-month card.

Does insurance cover blood pressure medication for truck drivers?

Most health plans cover generic blood pressure medications, often for a low copay, along with the doctor visits needed to prescribe and adjust them. The DOT physical itself usually isn't covered, but the care that keeps your blood pressure under 140/90 is.

What raises blood pressure right before a DOT physical?

Caffeine, nicotine, energy drinks, some cold and sinus decongestants, rushing in late, and anxiety in the exam room (white-coat effect) can all push a reading up. Take your regular medicine as prescribed, avoid stimulants beforehand, arrive early and sit quietly. Ask your own doctor before changing any medication.
